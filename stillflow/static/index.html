FROM ANNOTATIONS TO ANIMATION

Three algorithms. One loop.

  1. Interpolate a flow field. Each arrow describes a displacement. Inverse-distance weights blend the sparse arrows into a dense vector field. Anchors add soft zero-motion constraints.
  2. Warp the original image. Every output pixel samples its source at x − tD(x). A custom bilinear sampler combines the four surrounding pixels. Out-of-bounds coordinates clamp to the image edge.
  3. Make it loop. Near the end, smoothstep cross-fading blends two shifted phases. An inward-feathered mask keeps unpainted pixels exactly unchanged before GIF encoding.

Try it: load River rapids → Render loop → Preview. Return to Edit, change one arrow, and render again. Turn off loop blending to see the reset artifact. Save a project to keep the image, mask, arrows, and settings together.

Interpret the numbers: a seam ratio near 1 means the last-to-first change resembles a typical frame transition. It does not measure realism. No motion gives an undefined ratio. Cross-fading can still produce ghosting; large arrows stretch textures. A single image cannot reveal hidden content or infer true physical motion.

Examples are public-domain National Park Service photographs. Credits and exact sources are in examples/sources.json. No model, GPU, account, or internet connection is needed after installation.
